Weighted Grade Calculator — Live Course Tracker

A grade calculator is an essential academic tool that combines all of your assignment, quiz, project, and exam scores — each weighted according to your course syllabus — into one clear overall course grade. This free Grade Calculator from Scholarics is a fully weighted grade calculator: enter each item's score and its weight, and your current standing updates instantly, complete with a matching letter grade.

Tracking your grades throughout the term matters more than most students realize. Every course grade feeds directly into your GPA, which shapes eligibility for scholarships, honors programs, graduate admissions, and even some jobs. Checking your standing regularly turns a low midterm into an early warning instead of a bad surprise — you can see exactly how much each remaining assessment can help or hurt before it is too late to act.

Not every gradebook counts the same way. In an unweighted system, all assignments are simply averaged together. Most courses, however, use weighted grades, where a final exam worth 40% moves your grade far more than a homework set worth 10%. Knowing the difference is the key to studying strategically, because it tells you which assessments deserve your attention. A college grade calculator like this one handles that math automatically — and it stays accurate mid-term, when only some categories have been graded.

Students use an online grade calculator to estimate their final grade before official results are released: enter your known scores, add an expected score for the remaining exam, and calculate your grade in seconds. Quick Answer

A weighted grade calculator multiplies each assignment or exam score by its percentage weight, then sums the results. For example, if a midterm worth 30% scores 85 and a final worth 40% scores 90, those components contribute 25.5 and 36 points respectively to your weighted total.

How to Use This Grade Calculator

  1. The calculator opens with sample entries — replace them with your own, and click "Add item" or "Add another item" to create a row for every graded component in your course (homework, quizzes, midterm, final exam, projects, and so on).
  2. Optionally name each item, for example "Midterm Exam", so you can recognize it later.
  3. Enter your score for each item as a percentage. If your score is not out of 100, convert it first (18 out of 20 becomes 90).
  4. Enter each item's weight exactly as listed in your syllabus. For a final course grade, the weights should total 100%.
  5. Watch the results panel update live with your overall percentage and letter grade; the note below it tells you whether your weights are under, at, or over 100%.
  6. Use the Copy or PDF buttons in the results panel to save your grade. Your rows are stored automatically in your browser, so you can leave and pick up where you left off.

How Grades Are Calculated

Most courses grade you with a weighted average: every assessment contributes to your final grade in proportion to its weight. Multiply each score by its weight, then add the results together:

Final Grade = Σ(Assignment Score × Assignment Weight)

In simple terms, a score on a heavily weighted exam counts many times toward your grade, while a lightly weighted quiz counts only a little. When your weights add up to exactly 100%, the sum above is your final grade. If you have only completed part of the course, this calculator divides by the weight you have entered so far — Grade = Σ(Score × Weight) ÷ Σ(Weight) — giving your true average on work completed. In an unweighted course, every item simply gets the same weight, and the formula reduces to a plain average of your scores.

Example Grade Calculation

Suppose your syllabus weights the course like this, and these are your scores:

Component Weight Score Weighted Points
Homework 25% 90% 22.5
Midterm 35% 82% 28.7
Final Exam 40% 88% 35.2
Final Grade 86.4%

The calculation: (90 × 0.25) + (82 × 0.35) + (88 × 0.40) = 22.5 + 28.7 + 35.2 = 86.4%, which corresponds to a B on the common letter scale below. Notice how the 82% midterm barely dents the final result — its 35% weight softens the blow — while the strong 40%-weighted final exam pulls the average up. If the final exam had not happened yet, entering just the first two rows (60% of the weight) would still show your accurate mid-term average of 85.3%.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Grade Calculator?

A grade calculator is an online tool that combines your assignment, quiz, and exam scores — each with its own weight — into a single overall course grade. The Scholarics Grade Calculator updates your weighted average live as you type and shows the matching letter grade.

How are weighted grades calculated?

Multiply each score by its weight, add all the results together, and divide by the total weight: Grade = Σ(Score × Weight) ÷ Σ(Weight). Your weights do not have to total 100% — if the total is lower, the calculator shows your average for the work completed so far.

Can I calculate my final grade?

Yes. Enter every course component — including the final exam with an estimated score — and make sure the weights total 100%. If you want to know the exact final-exam score needed for a target grade, use the Scholarics Final Grade Calculator.

Is this calculator accurate?

Yes. It uses the standard weighted-average formula that instructors themselves apply, and it normalizes your weights automatically. Enter scores and weights exactly as they appear in your course syllabus for a precise result.

Can I calculate percentages?

Yes — the tool works entirely in percentages. If a score is not out of 100, convert it first: for 18 marks out of 20, compute (18 ÷ 20) × 100 = 90 and enter 90 as the score.

Is it free?

Yes. The Scholarics Grade Calculator is completely free, with no signup, no account, and no limits. Your entries are saved in your own browser for convenience.

Can I use it on mobile?

Yes. The calculator is fully responsive and works smoothly on smartphones, tablets, and desktop computers — no app installation is needed.

Does it work for all grading systems?

It supports any course graded with weighted percentage categories — the most common system in schools and universities. If your institution uses a different scheme, convert each mark to a percentage first. The built-in letter grade follows a common US-style scale, and your syllabus always takes precedence.
