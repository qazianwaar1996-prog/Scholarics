Percentage to GPA Converter

Percentage to GPA conversion turns the marks on your transcript into the grade point average format that another institution or country understands. This free Percentage to GPA Calculator from Scholarics does it in one step: enter your percentage, choose a target scale — US 4.0, Pakistan 4.0, India 10-point, Nigeria 5.0, or UK classification — and the equivalent GPA appears instantly with its letter grade and class.

Students need this because universities around the world grade differently. The US standard is a percentage to 4.0 GPA scale, Nigeria commonly reports a 5.0 scale, Indian universities publish 10-point CGPA results, and many systems in the UK, South Asia, and elsewhere still work in plain percentages. There is no universal conversion formula between them: every institution publishes its own bands, tables, or shortcuts, so any converter — including this one — provides a standard-reference estimate rather than an official evaluation.

That estimate is exactly what most students need at the decision stage. Admissions forms for US colleges ask for a 4.0-scale GPA, scholarship applications screen on minimum GPA cutoffs, and international applications often require converted figures long before formal credential evaluation. Studying in India means thinking in reverse terms too, since a percentage to 10 GPA figure is what many Indian portals expect. A dependable GPA converter saves students from rough mental math during these time-sensitive steps.

Whether you use this page as a quick CGPA converter or to double-check a single semester's percentage, Scholarics keeps it completely free and requires no signup. Your last entry stays saved in your own browser, the conversion tables for every supported scale are listed right below the tool, and you can convert percentage to GPA as often as you like, on any device.

How the Conversion Works

Each GPA scale divides the 0–100% range into grade bands, and your percentage is placed into the band it falls in. For example, on the US 4.0 scale, an 85% lands in the 80–89% band, which maps to a B (3.0); on the India 10-point scale, the same 85% falls in the 81–90% band, giving an A+ (9.0). Different universities sometimes use slightly different band edges, so treat the result as a standard-reference estimate rather than an official evaluation.

Why There Is No Universal Percentage to GPA Formula

Students often search for a single equation, but one does not exist. There is no universal percentage-to-GPA formula: every university chooses its own conversion method. Some publish band tables, where a range of percentages maps to one letter grade and GPA value. Some use linear shortcuts — a rough percentage ÷ 25 estimate for the 4.0 scale, or the CGPA × 9.5 shortcut used by parts of the Indian system. Others evaluate transcripts course by course instead of converting a single number.

That is why this calculator asks you to select a scale first. It then follows the standard reference band table of the scale you selected — US 4.0, Pakistan 4.0, India 10-point, Nigeria 5.0, or UK Classification — and shows exactly which band your percentage lands in. Treat the result as an informed, widely accepted estimate; for official admissions or credential evaluations, the policy of your target institution always takes precedence.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert percentage to GPA?

Choose the target GPA scale used by the institution you are applying to — for example US 4.0, India 10-point, or Nigeria 5.0 — and enter your percentage. The calculator places your score into that scale's grade band and shows the equivalent GPA and letter instantly.

Is there one standard conversion formula?

No. There is no universal percentage-to-GPA formula: universities publish their own conversion tables and shortcuts (some Indian institutions, for instance, use CGPA × 9.5 to move between CGPA and percentage). This calculator follows the widely used band tables for each selected scale, so always confirm the official method of your target institution.

Does every university use the same GPA scale?

No, and that is exactly why converters exist. The US commonly uses 4.0, India a 10-point CGPA, Nigeria a 5.0 scale, the UK degree classifications, and many countries still report plain percentages. Even between universities in the same country, band edges and equivalences can differ.

What is a 4.0 GPA scale?

The 4.0 scale is the grading standard common in the United States: letter grades convert to grade points from 0.0 (F) up to 4.0 (A), and course averages are weighted by credit hours. Some institutions extend it with plus/minus values such as 3.7 for an A−.

Can I convert CGPA to GPA?

Yes. To convert a 10-point CGPA to a 4.0-style GPA, a common approximation is (CGPA ÷ 10) × 4, but policies differ and many universities ask for band-by-band conversion instead, so check your target institution's rule before you apply.

Is this calculator accurate?

It accurately applies the standard reference band table of the scale you select (US 4.0, Pakistan 4.0, India 10-point, Nigeria 5.0, or UK classification). Because institutions set their own conversion policies, treat results as a standard-reference estimate and verify the target institution's policy for official applications.
