How the Conversion Works

Each GPA scale divides the 0–100% range into grade bands, and your percentage is placed into the band it falls in. For example, on the US 4.0 scale, an 85% lands in the 80–89% band, which maps to a B (3.0); on the India 10-point scale, the same 85% falls in the 81–90% band, giving an A+ (9.0). Different universities sometimes use slightly different band edges, so treat the result as a standard-reference estimate rather than an official evaluation.

Is the conversion the same at every university?

Not exactly. Many universities publish their own mapping tables (for example, some Indian institutions use the "CGPA × 9.5" shortcut instead of band mapping). This tool uses the most common band tables, but for official applications check the specific conversion policy of your target university.

What does a UK classification mean in GPA terms?

A UK First (70%+) is commonly treated as a US 3.7–4.0, a 2:1 (60–69%) as 3.0–3.6, and a 2:2 (50–59%) as roughly 2.7–3.0. See the UK grading system guide for details.

Which scale should I convert to?

Convert to the scale used by the country where you're applying — US universities expect a 4.0 GPA, Indian institutions a 10-point CGPA, and so on. Use the Global GPA Converter if you need more than one scale at once.
