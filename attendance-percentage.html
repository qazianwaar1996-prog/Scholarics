Attendance Percentage Formula

Attendance % = (Classes Attended ÷ Total Classes Held) × 100

For example: if 42 classes have been held and you attended 36, your attendance percentage is (36 ÷ 42) × 100 = 85.7%. This is the figure your university tracks against its minimum requirement.

Why attendance percentage matters

Most universities with a mandatory attendance policy set the threshold at 75% or 80%. Falling below it typically bars you from sitting the final examination for that subject — regardless of how well you have done on assignments and mid-terms. Some institutions allow a grace period called "condonation" for students in the 65–74% range, but this is not guaranteed and often requires a fee or documentation.

Beyond the exam eligibility threshold, attendance also correlates with grade performance. Students who attend more classes consistently earn higher marks, partly because they hear emphasis cues that don't appear in notes, and partly because consistent attendance builds the study habits that carry over to exam preparation.

How to track your attendance across subjects

The biggest mistake students make is tracking only their overall average — which can hide a danger-zone subject. Track each subject separately. Use this calculator to check your current percentage per subject, then use the Attendance Calculator to see how many more classes you can safely miss (or must attend to recover).

Does attendance affect my grade?

At most institutions, attendance below the minimum threshold bars you from sitting final exams entirely — regardless of your assignment or test performance. Some courses also award participation marks directly tied to attendance. Check your syllabus for both the exam-eligibility threshold and any direct mark deductions for absences.

What counts as a class?

Lectures, tutorials, labs, and practicals are all typically counted separately. A two-hour lab session and a one-hour lecture may each count as one class toward the attendance record, or may be weighted by duration — check your department's policy. Online synchronous sessions usually count the same as in-person attendance; recorded sessions typically do not.

Can I recover from low attendance mid-semester?

Yes, if enough classes remain. Use the Attendance Goal Calculator — enter your current attended and total classes, plus your target percentage, and it tells you exactly how many of the remaining sessions you must attend. If the math says recovery is impossible (not enough classes left), contact your academic advisor immediately about condonation options.

Do medical absences get excluded from the attendance count?

Usually yes — if you submit documentation within the required timeframe. Most universities allow medically certified absences to be formally excused and removed from the calculation. The process typically requires a medical certificate submitted within 7–14 days of the absence. Do not wait until end of semester to file — retroactive requests are usually denied.
