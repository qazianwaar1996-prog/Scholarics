The GPA Scale, Explained

What a 4.0 GPA actually means, how letter grades map to numbers, and the exact formula used to calculate it — in plain English.

Quick Answer

The 4.0 GPA scale converts letter grades to grade points: A = 4.0, B = 3.0, C = 2.0, D = 1.0, F = 0.0. GPA is calculated by multiplying each grade point by its credit hours, summing all quality points, then dividing by total credit hours attempted.

What is the GPA scale?

GPA stands for Grade Point Average. It is a single number — typically between 0.0 and 4.0 in the US — that summarises your academic performance by converting letter grades into numbers, multiplying by credit hours, and dividing by total credits attempted. Universities use it to evaluate academic standing, determine scholarships, flag students for academic probation, and compare candidates during graduate school admissions.

The scale originated in the early 20th century as universities sought a standardised way to compress semester-by-semester performance into a single comparable metric. Today it is the dominant grading summary used across US, Canadian, and many Asian universities — though international equivalents like the UK's degree classification system or India's 10-point CGPA scale work on different numbers.

The standard US conversion table is:

B−80–82%2.7Good

Note: not every university uses plus/minus grades. Some award only A, B, C, D, or F, mapping each to 4.0, 3.0, 2.0, 1.0, and 0.0 respectively. Always check your institution's official grade-point table — a B+ worth 3.3 at one school may be worth 3.5 at another.

The GPA formula, step by step

For each course, multiply its grade points by the number of credit hours. Sum all of those products (the total "quality points"), then divide by the total credit hours attempted:

GPA = Σ (Grade Points × Credit Hours) ÷ Σ Credit Hours

Let's walk through a real example. Suppose you took four courses this semester:

Course Grade Grade Points Credit Hours Quality Points
Biology 101A4.0312.0
Math 202B+3.3413.2
English 105A−3.7311.1
History 110C+2.324.6
Total——1240.9

GPA = 40.9 ÷ 12 = 3.41

Weighted vs. unweighted GPA

Unweighted GPA uses the same 4.0 ceiling for every course regardless of difficulty. An A in an introductory elective and an A in Advanced Placement Chemistry are both worth 4.0. This is the standard at virtually all US colleges and universities.

Weighted GPA, most commonly used in US high schools, adds bonus grade points for harder courses. A typical weighted scale might award:

  • Regular courses: standard 4.0 scale
  • Honors courses: +0.5 bonus (so A = 4.5)
  • AP / IB courses: +1.0 bonus (so A = 5.0)

A student with a 4.2 weighted GPA might have a 3.9 unweighted GPA. When colleges review applications, many recalculate GPAs on an unweighted basis to compare students fairly across different high school grading policies.

At university, your cumulative GPA across all semesters is always unweighted — use the CGPA Calculator to see yours across multiple terms.

Semester GPA vs. cumulative GPA

Semester GPA (or term GPA) covers only the courses from one term. Cumulative GPA covers every course you've taken at the institution to date. The cumulative figure is what appears on your transcript and what employers and graduate schools look at.

Because cumulative GPA is a weighted average across all credits, early semesters have a lasting drag. A 2.5 earned in your freshman year takes many semesters to dilute, even if you earn 4.0s afterward. This is why students in academic difficulty are often advised to act fast — the math compounds against you. GPA on a 5.0 or 10-point scale

Not all countries use 4.0. The 10-point scale is common at Indian universities (where it's called CGPA), the 5.0 scale appears in parts of the Middle East and some US high schools, and many European countries use a 20-point or percentage-based system. If you're converting your GPA for international applications, the Global GPA Converter handles 30+ country systems in both directions.

GPA and Latin honours

US universities typically award Latin honours at graduation based on cumulative GPA:

  • Cum Laude ("with praise"): usually 3.5–3.69
  • Magna Cum Laude ("with great praise"): usually 3.7–3.89
  • Summa Cum Laude ("with highest praise"): usually 3.9–4.0

Thresholds vary by institution. Some schools set them based on a percentage of graduating students rather than fixed GPA cutoffs, so always verify your university's specific policy.

Frequently asked questions

What does a 4.0 GPA mean?

A 4.0 is the highest possible GPA on the standard US scale. It means you received an A (90–100%) in every credit-bearing course. The number comes from the grade-point value assigned to each letter: A = 4.0, B = 3.0, C = 2.0, D = 1.0, F = 0.

Can you have a GPA above 4.0?

Yes — on a weighted scale. Many high schools award bonus grade points for honors, AP, or IB courses (an A in an AP class might count as 5.0 instead of 4.0). College transcripts almost always use the unweighted 4.0 scale, so GPAs above 4.0 are rare in higher education.

How do plus and minus grades affect GPA?

A+ and A are both worth 4.0 in most systems, but A− is 3.7, B+ is 3.3, B is 3.0, B− is 2.7, and so on. Some schools do not use plus/minus grading — check your institution's grade-point table before calculating.

What is the minimum GPA to graduate?

Most US universities require a cumulative GPA of at least 2.0 (a C average) to earn a degree. Individual programs, especially competitive ones like engineering or nursing, often require 2.5 or 3.0. Graduate schools typically require 3.0.

Do failed or retaken courses affect GPA?

Yes. An F (0.0) is included in the GPA calculation and drags the average down significantly. When you retake a course, some institutions replace the original grade with the new one (grade forgiveness/replacement), while others average both attempts. Check your registrar's retake policy before assuming a repeat will erase a bad grade.

Does GPA include pass/fail courses?

Usually not. Pass/Fail or Credit/No-Credit courses are typically excluded from the GPA calculation, though they still appear on your transcript. They do count toward total credits earned.

How long does it take to raise a low GPA?

It depends on how many credits you've already completed. Early in your degree, one excellent semester can move the needle significantly.
